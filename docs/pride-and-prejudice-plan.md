# Pride and Prejudice: chapter plan

The baseline for how many levels the first book needs, so the story stays clear for someone who has never read it.

## The book in numbers

- **61 chapters**, about **122,000 words** (about 2,000 per chapter), in three volumes: chapters 1–23, 24–42 and 43–61.
- **69 captioned illustrations** by Hugh Thomson (1894) placed in the text (Project Gutenberg ebook #1342). The full 1894 edition has about 160, counting chapter headings and small vignettes.
- **18 chapters have no captioned illustration** (4, 9, 11–13, 15, 17, 19, 24, 29, 31, 33, 35, 39, 43, 45, 55, 60). Some of them hold key scenes, such as the letter in chapter 35. Those levels use a neighbouring illustration or a heading vignette.

## How much to cover

Each level retells one scene in about 60 words and hides 6–9 words. One level takes about 3–5 minutes.

| Tier | Levels | Playtime | What it gives the player |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Core** | **37** | about 2–3 h | Every turn of the main plot: the two couples, Wickham's lie and its exposure, both proposals, the elopement, the happy endings. Someone who never read the book can follow it. |
| **Full** | **48** | about 3–4 h | Core plus favourite comic and character scenes (Mr. Bennet's verdict, Mary at the ball, Lady Catherine's dinner…). |
| One per chapter | 61+ | 4–5 h | Not worth it: many chapters are letters or reflection with little to show, and the pace would sag. |

**Baseline: the 37 core levels, plus the full-tier scenes we like best, for about 40 levels.** That's one level for every ~1.5 chapters, and a satisfying pack for a single purchase.

Built (2026-10-02): **38 levels**, the whole story. Every core scene is in, some merged (Bingley leaving London folds into Charlotte's engagement; the letter is two levels; the second proposal and telling Jane are one), plus the full-tier scenes for chapters 9, 20 and 31. Scenes without a Thomson drawing on Commons use C. E. Brock's 1895 plates.

Updated (2026-10-03): **42 levels**. Four turning points added so the story holds together for a first-time reader: the sisters keep Wickham's secret (ch. 40), Elizabeth sees Darcy is exactly the man for her (ch. 50), Darcy's side of the second proposal (ch. 58: Lady Catherine taught him to hope, he set Bingley right) and what became of everyone (ch. 61). Chapter 45's "one of the handsomest women of my acquaintance" joins the Miss Darcy passage in plain text. Thomson's drawings now come from the complete Gutenberg edition, served from public/art.

## Notes for writing levels

- **Names before blanks:** a name can only be hidden after it has appeared in plain text (the tests enforce this). Plan who gets introduced where: Charlotte in level 5, Wickham in 12, Georgiana in 28, the Gardiners in 20 (or 32 if 20 is cut).
- **Grid sizes:** keep 6×7 to 7×8. Make the big emotional scenes (the proposals, the letter, Pemberley) the bigger boards.
- **Free demo:** a natural cut is after level 19 (Charlotte's engagement, end of Volume I) or level 12 (Wickham appears). A shorter free sample (6–8 levels) leaves the player curious about Darcy.
- **Effort:** about 20–30 minutes per level (choose the scene, write the passage, pick the illustration, generate). 40 levels comes to about 15–20 hours of content work.
